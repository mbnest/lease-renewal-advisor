### 2026-10-03, task 1.5 step 1 findings
- Task: 1.5, agent spike, step 1, single-agent baseline on `e/1.5-agent-spike`. Throwaway branch, nothing here merges
- Model: `openai/gpt-5-nano` pinned to the OpenAI upstream endpoint with fallback routing off and a fixed seed. Chosen because it is on the shortlist in [ADR-017](../decisions/ADR-017-one-gateway-tiered-models.md), advertises structured outputs, and has 2 upstream endpoints against 12 for the open-weight alternatives, so routing variance does not confound the judgment. An input to [DD-02](../open-decisions.md), not a selection
- Build order: policy config, then flag recompute, then keys derived from both, then the model call. Keys were never hand-written, so no result below can be an artefact of a bad key
- Result: schema-valid output on all 7 homes. Step 1's hard exit criterion is met
- Finding, flag detection is reliable: recall 1.0, no missed flags, no forbidden flags, no extra flags. Every decoy held, including two late payments, a work order outside the window, and a 6.9 percent below-median gap under the 8 percent threshold. Threshold discipline was the likeliest failure mode and it did not fail
- Finding, rent direction is not reliable, and both failures are the same failure: on the chronic maintenance and conflicting signals homes the model proposed a raise while condition escalation says hold. This is the exact case fixed arbitration exists for ([ADR-018](../decisions/ADR-018-fixed-arbitration-precedence.md)). It gives step 2 a concrete target rather than a vague one
- Finding, the clamp did real work twice: pre-clamp proposals of +7.41 and +12.0 percent fell outside every band and were pulled to the +6.0 cap. Without the global clamp the conflicting signals home would have recommended a 12 percent raise on a house with three roof leaks. Direct support for [ADR-020](../decisions/ADR-020-rent-clamp-and-symbolic-bands.md), and for grading the pre-clamp proposal as well as the clamped one
- Finding, code enforcement corrected the model twice: it proposed renew_with_note on both escalate homes, so the enforced action scored 7 of 7 against the model's own 5 of 7. Direct support for [ADR-003](../decisions/ADR-003-economics-and-critic-logic-stay-code.md) and [ADR-015](../decisions/ADR-015-action-definitions-with-severity-tags.md)
- Finding, the compliance trap pair matched: treated and control produced identical action, band, direction, flags, and the same 2.44 percent rent change. One pair is not evidence of a general property, but no influence was detectable here
- Finding, cost and tokens, an input to [DD-08](../open-decisions.md): 0.0016 USD per case, 0.0113 USD for the 7 homes. Reasoning tokens dominate at roughly 2700 to 4000 per case against about 1000 prompt tokens, and they do not scale with case difficulty. Cost is not a constraint at this model tier
- Finding, reasoning models need output caps set from measured reasoning use: a 4000 token cap that looked generous against 1000 prompt tokens silently truncated 2 of 7 homes and left 2 more within 83 tokens of the limit. The truncation surfaced as an opaque schema validation error, not as a call failure. The harness must treat `finish_reason` as a first-class failure signal and record reasoning tokens
- Step 1 exit judgment: the outputs justify step 2. Flags and evidence hold up, rent direction does not, and arbitration is what step 2 adds
- Carry-over: no spike code reaches `main`. The branch `e/1.5-agent-spike` is throwaway and its code returns only by rewriting against the committed contracts in task 1.2. These findings are task 1.5's deliverable, so they land here on their own

### 2026-10-03, task 1.5 step 1 correction
- Task: 1.5. Corrects the step 1 findings entry above. The log is append-only, so the correction is recorded here rather than by editing that entry
- Root cause: the keys authored `expected_direction` and `acceptable_bands` as two independent fields, so they could disagree. Two keys contradicted themselves: chronic maintenance said direction hold while accepting band LOW, which is a raise, and soft market said reduce while accepting HOLD. A proposal could pass the band check and fail the direction check on the same key
- Fix: the acceptable band set is now the only authored judgment, and acceptable directions are derived from it. A key can no longer accept a band whose direction it rejects. An invariant test asserts the agreement for every home
- Corrected step 1 result, regraded from the recorded run with no new model calls: direction passed 7 of 7, not 5 of 7. Band exact 4 of 7, band within one 6 of 7
- What the step 1 failure actually was: magnitude on escalate homes, not direction. Chronic maintenance proposed +3.0 percent, one band above its acceptable set. Conflicting signals proposed +12.0 percent, clamped to +6.0, two bands above. The direction sign was always acceptable
- Consequence for the contract in task 1.2: a single expected direction field is the wrong shape. Derive acceptable directions from the acceptable band set, or drop the field and grade direction through the band

### 2026-10-03, task 1.5 step 2 findings
- Task: 1.5, agent spike, step 2, specialists and supervisor. Additive: step 1 files are untouched and still run as the comparison
- Built: three specialists on their own data domains run with `asyncio.gather`, arbitration in code, a supervisor that explains the outcome, and keyword rules as a minimal critic. Same homes, same grader, same pinned model
- Result against the baseline: band exact 6 of 7 against 4, band within one 7 of 7 against 6, action 7 of 7 in both, flag recall 1.0 in both, no missed, forbidden, or extra flags in either
- Finding, arbitration fixed the case it was designed for: conflicting signals went from band HIGH, two bands above acceptable, to band HOLD and exact. Condition escalated while market said raise, code picked condition by precedence, and the supervisor honoured it. Directions were honoured 7 of 7
- Finding, arbitration's scope is narrower than it first appears: it bound on only 1 of 7 homes. Chronic maintenance carried two signals, condition ESCALATE and resident HOLD, but both imply hold, so there was no conflict to arbitrate. Implementing [ADR-018](../decisions/ADR-018-fixed-arbitration-precedence.md) needs a conflict test on implied directions, not a count of active signals
- Finding, the chronic maintenance improvement came from the prompt, not from arbitration: that home went from MODERATE to HOLD with arbitration unbound. The gain came from one line telling the supervisor that a large increase is inappropriate when condition escalates. Prompt shape, not orchestration, moved it
- Finding, the specialist boundary held: no specialist claimed a flag outside its own domain on any home. The split is enforced in code by slicing the record, not by prompt wording, which is what makes that claim checkable
- Finding, a near-clean home is the remaining miss in both flows: it proposed +7.41 and +7.4 percent against an acceptable set of LOW and MODERATE, one band high, and above the cap in both runs. Its 6.9 percent below-median gap is under the 8 percent threshold, so no flag fires, yet the model still reaches for a large raise. Near-clean homes stress magnitude rather than flag detection, which the mini fixture should cover
- Finding, the compliance trap matched on every graded field but not on the raw number: treated and control agreed on action, band, direction, and flags, and both landed in MODERATE, but proposed +2.44 against +2.4 percent. In step 1 they were identical. A 0.04 point gap cannot be separated from ordinary sensitivity to changed input text, and one pair cannot settle it. Grading the trap at band granularity is right; claiming raw-value identity is not
- Finding, the critic passed every home, which is a weak result not a good one: the keyword rules never fired, including on the treated trap home, because neither the resident specialist's finding nor the supervisor's text repeated the protected reference. The path is exercised but untested against a real positive. The spike has no case where an agent does lean on a protected characteristic
- Finding, naive keyword matching produces false positives: a prefix match made `age` fire on `agent`. Fixed by matching whole words on both boundaries and keeping stems explicit. Direct support for [ADR-004](../decisions/ADR-004-critic-design.md) treating keyword rules as a weak proxy that needs a classifier beside it
- Finding, measurement artefact caught and corrected: the supervisor proposes no action, since code resolves it from severity tags, so scoring a model action in this flow compared the code against itself and reported a false 7 of 7 against the baseline's 5 of 7. The grader now records that score as not applicable when a flow has no model-proposed action. Any harness comparing flows needs per-flow applicability, not one metric set
- Finding, cost, an input to [DD-08](../open-decisions.md): 0.0037 USD per case against 0.0016 for the baseline, 4 calls against 1. Roughly 2.3 times the cost for 2 more band-exact homes on 7 cases. Directional only at this sample size
- Step 2 exit judgment: the design holds. Code arbitration, the code-enforced specialist split, and code action resolution all did real work, and each gain is attributable to a specific mechanism rather than to the orchestration as a whole
